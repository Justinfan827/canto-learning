## Brand analysis

### Babbel: a "personal language coach" that looks like a magazine
- **Mascot / characters:** **none, by design.** The 2024 rebrand (agency **Koto**, unveiled 1 Feb 2024) gives Babbel the personality of an "approachable, professional, optimistic" coach. People appear only as **photography** of real learners and teachers (café table, stairs, sofa, a teacher on video).
- **Illustration style:** no drawn illustration. Three devices replace it:
  1. **Photography** with lesson labels laid over it as product cards (`photo-card-talk-about-food`, `photo-lesson-introduce-yourself`).
  2. **Cut-out photographed objects** (coffee cup, picnic basket, saxophone, bicycle) collaged on flat colour blocks (`hero-lesson-card-collage`, `blog/medium-babbeldesign-rebrand-hero-cutout-collage.png`).
  3. **Monochrome orange silhouettes** of objects as a pattern (`blog/medium-babbeldesign-rebrand-new-look-cutout-objects.png`).

  Line icons are thin, rounded and dark brown (`icon-lightbulb`, `icon-medal`, `icon-users`). Babbel's design team wrote that AI-generated lesson images "fell short" on consistency, so they art-directed the top 300 lessons by hand.
- **Colour:** warm, print-like. A **cream canvas `#F6F4EF`** (not white), near-black ink `#151515`, and **Babbel orange `#FE4D01`** kept from the old brand: logo, Sign up, the "Over 25 million subscriptions sold!" strip, the closing CTA band, and *one italic word per headline* ("Which *language* do you want to learn?", "Babbel and *beyond*"). Big **butter-yellow `#FFD966`** bands carry the method and reviews. Lesson and skill tiles add forest green `#3E522D`, salmon `#FE9567` and purple `#671EB0`.
- **Shape and type:** **Feature Text**, a high-contrast serif in **regular 400** (40px headings, normal tracking, sentence case). This is the one brand here whose headlines are not heavy. Body and UI use **Graphik Babbel**. Buttons are **pills** (`border-radius: 4000px`, 44px, no shadow, regular weight); the primary "Start learning" is **black**, not orange, and orange is reserved for Sign up. Language choices are white pills with a round flag.
- **Voice:** grown-up, outcome-focused and lightly witty: "The effective way to learn a language online", "It's like having a private tutor in your pocket", "It'll only change your world. No big deal." (campaign). Claims are backed by study footnotes ("92% of users improved their proficiency level…").
- **Habit and reward (as marketed):** "Learn at your own pace", progress trackers, the "Babbel Method". There are no streak or gamification visuals on the homepage.
- **Chinese / Cantonese specifics:** none. Babbel doesn't teach Chinese.
